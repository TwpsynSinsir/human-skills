### Bias in the Tech Sector

DEI in the tech sector.

What are the benefits of a more diverse tech sector? For the organisations/businesses/creators? For individuals & groups?

If a company disciminates against people based on factors unrelated to their work abilities, they would be missing out on
potentially skilled workers.

The more diverse the individuals working in tech, the greater the diversity of products being supported and produced.
Making DEI initiatives profitable for the businesses and organisations is vital to bring it into the norm in all sectors.

If the workforce biodiversity is low, the industry stagnates with less sources of innovation.

### Journaling Exercise

#### What has helped form my implicit biases?

Growing up in a rural area. Living in the UK, Japan, and NZ.

#### What unconscious biases might I hold?

English proficiency of different groups. Group identity is a stronger bias than people recognise.

#### What biases might other people hold about me?

Straight White Guy - Maybe people think I hold biases against other groups?

#### What privileges do I have that others may not?

English first language, supportive family.

#### Do I interact with people that have different lived experiences than me?

I would say so, I have plenty of friends from different countries, ethnicities, genders.

#### What feelings and/or thoughts come up when thinking about this?

I haven't felt that I've had much of a benefit due to my upbringing. I only felt my privilege based on skin colour
when I went to a non-white majority country. I think a lack of money and growing up rurally has had more of an effect
than other factors.

I've seen group identity and tribalism affect people's decision making more than the traditional idea of discrimination
based on gender/race. i.e. People will usually favour people who look like them or are from the same area as them,
whether that's culturally, ethnically, nationally, or politically.
